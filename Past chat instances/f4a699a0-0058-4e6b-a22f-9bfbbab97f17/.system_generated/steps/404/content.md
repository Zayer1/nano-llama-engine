  <meta name="description" content="I just hired 9 people at Reflow in two months. This one question, I asked every single one of them:

&quot;What is the hardest thing you've ever gone through in your life?&quot;

Not what's your biggest weakness, not where do you see yourself in five years, not walk me through your resume. This one.

Why? Read on.

Most people don't think about who they want or need before they hire. They generate a JD (with AI) and start interviewing. I've made that mistake (I still find myself doing).

I create an ideal team mate image in my mind first. Must haves, should haves, could haves. What I can't live without. I check with a couple of people- are these reasonable expectations? Is this the right role? Is this the right title? 

Then I design the interview process around those specific qualities. What are the best questions to get there? How can I test for these things? My golden rule there: No backward looking questions, always forward looking. It takes a lot of work. Every. Single. Time. 

But even the best process can’t show you who someone really is. That's where this question comes in: &quot;What is the hardest thing you've ever gone through in your life?&quot;

It's a really loaded question. Difficult to answer. And how someone handles that moment tells me more than anything else in the interview.

Have they been through something hard? How did they come out of it? Are they able to sit in front of a stranger and be honest about it?

I don't work with people who aren't nice. I don't work with people who don't have strong character. People who have been through hardships understand the value of hard work in a way that's very difficult to fake. Even in the age of AI. 

The process reveals the skills. That question reveals the character.

Skills I can work with. Character I can't help build. | 79 comments on LinkedIn">
